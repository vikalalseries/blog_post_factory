The Origins of Chess

Chess is generally believed to have originated in northern India during the Gupta Empire, around the 6th century CE. The early form of the game was called chaturanga, a Sanskrit word meaning "four divisions" of the military: infantry, cavalry, elephants, and chariots. These divisions evolved into the pieces we now know as the pawn, knight, bishop, and rook.

From India, the game spread to Persia, where it became known as shatranj. Persian players introduced the custom of warning an opponent when their king was under attack by saying "shah" (king), and the end of the game was announced with "shah mat," meaning "the king is helpless." This phrase is the origin of the English word "checkmate."

After the Islamic conquest of Persia in the 7th century, shatranj spread throughout the Muslim world. Arab scholars wrote some of the earliest known books on chess strategy and composed chess problems called mansubat. Strong players of this era, such as al-Adli and as-Suli, were celebrated at the courts of the Abbasid caliphs.

Chess reached Europe by the 10th century, arriving through Moorish Spain, Sicily, and trade routes with the Byzantine Empire. By the year 1000 it was being played in many parts of Western Europe, often as a pastime of the nobility.
